A little light is still light. Your brain reads the room all night.

Light passes through closed eyelids — your clock keeps receiving it long after you've shut your eyes. Even dim room light can blunt melatonin and push sleep later. Sound works the same way: noise triggers brief arousals you never remember, and the night quietly gets lighter.

What a normal bedroom does: standby LEDs, chargers and a streetlight through thin curtains — small sources, but they're on all night. The 3am phone check delivers a bright dose of light at the worst possible moment. And it isn't loudness that wakes you, it's the jump — in a silent room, every sound is a spike.

The reset: make the room boring.

— Dark enough that you can't see your hand. Blackout curtains, or a sleep mask — whichever one you'll actually keep using
— Cover or unplug the small lights. A single LED is worth taping over
— Steady sound beats silence. A fan or low white noise smooths out the spikes that wake you

A good room asks nothing of you.
