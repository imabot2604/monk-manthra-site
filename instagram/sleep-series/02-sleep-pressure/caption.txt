Exhausted, but not sleepy. There's a difference — and it's chemical.

Every waking hour, your brain accumulates adenosine, a by-product of simply being awake. The more it builds, the heavier the pull toward sleep. That pull is sleep pressure, and sleep is what clears it. Sixteen hours up and the tank is full — that's the design.

Caffeine doesn't remove that pressure. It hides it. It sits in the adenosine receptors so the signal can't land — the pressure is still there, you just can't feel it. And its half-life is roughly five to six hours, so a 4pm coffee is still half-working at 10pm. A late afternoon nap does the opposite: it vents the pressure you needed for bedtime.

The reset is simple, and none of it is a supplement:

— Last caffeine by early afternoon, so it has cleared by bedtime
— If you nap, keep it short and before 3pm
— Same wake time every day — pressure only builds on a fixed start line

Tired is a signal. Listen to it, don't mask it.
