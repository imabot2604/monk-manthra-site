Your body is ready. Your brain is still at work. The lights are off — the meeting is still on.

Quiet room, loud mind. Cortisol should be at its lowest at night, and that dip is part of what lets sleep begin. Under pressure it stays elevated, and a body on alert doesn't hand over to sleep. Bedtime is also the first quiet moment all day, so everything unprocessed arrives at once.

Thinking in bed teaches the bed to think. Work, worry or scroll there often enough and the bed becomes a cue for alertness instead of sleep. The last thing you read is the thing you lie there holding — news and inboxes included. And problem-solving at midnight rarely solves anything; it just keeps the system switched on.

The reset: put the day down before you lie down.

— Ten minutes, early evening, pen and paper: worries and tomorrow's list, out of your head
— Keep the bed for sleep. If you're thinking hard, do it in another room, then come back
— Slow the exhale. Longer out-breaths than in-breaths tell the body the threat is over

The day ends when you put it down. Not when you lie down.
