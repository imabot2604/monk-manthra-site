Your last meal is a sleep decision. Not just what — when.

Your gut has its own clock. The brain's clock runs on light, but the gut and liver run on food — every meal is a time signal. A big meal late says "it's still daytime" to half your body while the other half is trying to shut down. Digestion also keeps core temperature up, the exact opposite of the cool-down sleep needs.

What gets in the way: a heavy meal within two hours of bed, so you lie down while the body is still working. Sugar late at night, which spikes and then dips — and the dip can be what wakes you. And the other extreme: going to bed hungry keeps the brain alert, scanning for food.

The reset: eat earlier, lighter, and at the same time.

— Finish the main meal about three hours before bed. Warm, simple, not enormous
— Hungry later? Something small — a warm drink, a handful of nuts — not a second dinner
— Keep meal times consistent. Regular meals are as much a clock signal as regular light

Eat like it's evening. Your body will believe you.
