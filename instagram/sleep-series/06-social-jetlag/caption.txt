Every weekend, you change time zones — without leaving the house.

Your body clock is set by when you wake and when you first see light, not by the day of the week. Sleep in two hours on Sunday and you've shifted the clock two hours later. Like flying west. Then Sunday night arrives and you're not sleepy — because your body thinks it's 10pm at midnight.

Monday is the flight back. The alarm goes off at your weekday time, two hours before your body is ready. The grogginess isn't laziness — it's the same fog as landing from a long flight. Done every weekend, the clock never settles, and you spend Monday to Wednesday catching up.

The reset: stay in one time zone.

— Wake within an hour of your weekday time, even after a late night
— Tired? Nap early and short instead of lying in. A nap doesn't move the clock; a lie-in does
— Get outside soon after waking. Morning light is what pins the clock in place

The weekend is a break from work. Not from your clock.
