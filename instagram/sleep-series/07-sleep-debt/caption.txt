Sleep isn't a bank. You can't catch up in a night — but you can stop the debt from growing.

Short nights add up quietly. Lose an hour a night and by Friday you're five hours down: a whole night, spread thin. Focus, mood and reaction time keep sliding with every short night. The catch is that you stop noticing — sleepiness plateaus after a few days, performance doesn't.

Catching up isn't catching up. A Saturday lie-in restores some of it, not all, and only some of the sharpness comes back. It also shifts your clock later, so Sunday night is short again and the debt restarts on Monday. Five short nights and two long ones is a pattern, not a fix.

The reset: protect the weeknight, not the weekend.

— Move bedtime 20 minutes earlier on weeknights — small enough to keep, big enough to count
— Keep the wake time fixed, so the extra sleep lands at the front of the night, not the back
— Think in weeks, not nights. One good week beats one heroic Saturday

You don't repay sleep. You stop borrowing.
