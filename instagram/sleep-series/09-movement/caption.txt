A tired body is not a tired mind. Most bad nights begin with a still day.

Moving by day deepens the night. Daytime movement builds sleep pressure faster — the same pull that makes bedtime feel easy. It reliably increases deep sleep, the heaviest and most restorative stage of the night. And it burns off the day's stress hormones, so there's less left circulating when you lie down.

Where it backfires: hard training close to bed raises core temperature and adrenaline, and both say stay awake. A day spent sitting builds almost no sleep pressure — the body has nothing to recover from. And training to exhaustion to force sleep usually backfires, because that's stress, and stress is arousal.

The reset: move early, often, and outdoors.

— A morning walk does double duty: movement, plus the light that sets your clock
— Finish hard training about three hours before bed. Earlier is better than harder
— Evenings can still move — a slow walk or gentle stretching lowers arousal instead of raising it

Earn the night during the day.
