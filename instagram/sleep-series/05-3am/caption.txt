Wide awake at 3am? Nothing is wrong with you. Waking is normal — staying awake is the habit.

Sleep runs in cycles, not a line. Each night is a chain of roughly 90-minute cycles with a brief surfacing between them. The second half of the night is lighter — more dreaming, less deep sleep — so those surfacings get easier to notice. Most people wake several times and forget by morning. The waking isn't the problem. Noticing it is.

What turns a surfacing into an hour: alcohol, which knocks you out early and then wears off mid-night with a wide-awake rebound. Checking the time, which floods your eyes with light and starts the maths. And a warm, bright room, which tells your body the night is over hours before it is.

The reset: surface, but don't surface all the way.

— Don't look at the clock. Face it away — the time changes nothing you can act on
— Stay in the dark and breathe slow. The next cycle is already on its way
— No alcohol in the three hours before bed. The second half of the night will thank you

Waking at night is the body breathing. Let it exhale.
