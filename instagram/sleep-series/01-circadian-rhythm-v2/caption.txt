Tired at 2pm. Wired at 11pm. Your sleep clock isn't broken — it's just confused.

Here's what's actually happening: light hits your retina and travels straight to your suprachiasmatic nucleus — the brain's master clock. That's what shuts off melatonin production and tells the rest of your body the day has started. Core temperature and cortisol follow, right on schedule.

Evenings undo it just as easily. Screens and bright rooms at night delay melatonin release by hours, not minutes — so your internal clock drifts later, night after night, without you noticing. Tired mornings, wired nights. The cycle repeats itself.

Three things actually move it, and none of them are a supplement:

— Morning sunlight, within the first hour of waking
— A consistent wake time, even on weekends
— Dim light and no screens in the hour before bed

Rhythm first. Supplements second. Never the other way around.
