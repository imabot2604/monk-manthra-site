Too hot to sleep? It's not the weather. The body cools down before it switches off.

To fall asleep, your core temperature has to drop — by roughly a degree. Your body does it by sending heat out to the hands and feet, where it escapes through the skin. That drop is one of the strongest signals that it's time to sleep. No drop, no signal.

Anything that keeps you warm keeps you up. A warm bedroom or heavy bedding traps the heat you're trying to lose. A late workout or a big late meal raises core temperature right when it should be falling. Alcohol feels warming because it opens the skin's vessels — then disrupts the second half of the night.

The reset: cool room, warm hands and feet.

— Keep the bedroom cool, around 18 to 20°C, and let the air move
— A warm shower an hour or two before bed pulls heat to the skin, so your core drops faster afterwards
— Cold feet? Wear socks. Warm extremities dump heat; cold ones hold it in

The body cools before the mind does. Let it go first.
