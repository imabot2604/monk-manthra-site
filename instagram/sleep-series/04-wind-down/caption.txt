Sleep isn't a switch. It's a landing. You can't force it — you can only clear the runway.

Falling asleep is a gradual downshift: heart rate, breathing and brain activity all slow together. It's driven by the parasympathetic system, the body's rest-and-repair mode — and that mode needs a runway, a stretch of quiet before the lights go out.

What keeps you in the air: working or scrolling right up to bedtime, which holds the brain in alert mode and then asks it to stop instantly. Trying hard to sleep, which backfires — effort is arousal, and arousal is the opposite of sleep. And watching the clock, which turns every minute awake into a reason to stay awake.

The reset: clear the runway, the same way, every night.

— A 30-minute wind-down in the same order each night — the repetition itself becomes the signal
— Write tomorrow's list early, so it isn't waiting for you in the dark
— Awake for more than 20 minutes? Get up, keep the lights dim, come back when you're sleepy

You don't fall asleep. You let yourself land.
