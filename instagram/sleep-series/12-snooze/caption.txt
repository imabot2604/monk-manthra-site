Nine more minutes costs more than nine. Snoozing doesn't add sleep — it breaks it.

Waking foggy has a name: sleep inertia. For most people it clears in 15 to 30 minutes, and it isn't a sign you needed more sleep — it's the handover from sleep to waking, happening on schedule. Light and movement clear it faster than lying still ever will.

Nine minutes is not a sleep cycle. Each snooze starts a cycle you have no time to finish, so you get fragmented sleep rather than extra sleep. You can even end up waking from a deeper point than the first alarm did, which feels worse, not better. And the last stretch of the night is light and dream-rich — exactly the part snoozing chops up.

The reset: one alarm, then light.

— Set the alarm for when you actually get up, not twenty minutes of negotiation earlier
— Open the curtains first. Morning light clears the fog and sets tonight's clock at the same time
— Move within a few minutes: stand, stretch, walk to water. Inertia lifts with motion

The fog is not a reason to stay. It's the way out.
